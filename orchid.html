All About Orchids

Orchids belong to the family Orchidaceae, one of the two largest families of flowering plants on Earth. They are celebrated worldwide for their exotic appearance, structural symmetry, and incredible geometric complexity, making them a fascinating subject for both botanists and casual enthusiasts.

Incredible Adaptability

Orchids grow on almost every continent, adapting to environments ranging from tropical rainforests to alpine meadows. A unique characteristic of many tropical orchids is that they are epiphytes—meaning they grow anchored to trees or rocks instead of sitting directly in soil. They absorb moisture and nutrients straight from the air using specialized, spongy root systems.

Floral Structure & Design

What makes an orchid distinct is its highly evolved structural symmetry. Every orchid bloom features a modified lower petal known as the labellum or lip. This unique petal acts as a perfect landing pad for specific pollinators. The symmetry of the flower means that if you slice it vertically down the middle, the two halves mirror each other perfectly.

Care & Houseplant Tips

While orchids have a reputation for being delicate, the popular Moth Orchid (Phalaenopsis) makes an excellent houseplant. They prefer bright, indirect sunlight and a loose potting medium like tree bark or sphagnum moss rather than traditional dirt. The golden rule of orchid care is to let their roots dry out slightly between waterings to ensure proper airflow.
