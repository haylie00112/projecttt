All About Tulips

Tulips are iconic spring-blooming perennials belonging to the genus Tulipa. Celebrated globally for their simple, elegant cup-shaped petals and bright color profiles, they stand as one of the definitive heralds of the spring season.

The Ottoman Origins & Tulip Mania

While frequently associated with the Netherlands today, tulips were originally cultivated and highly prized in the Ottoman Empire. Introduced to Europe in the 16th century, their popularity skyrocketed, eventually leading to a famous economic phenomenon known as "Tulip Mania" in the 1630s, where rare tulip bulbs were traded as a form of high-value currency.

Botanical Characteristics

Tulips grow from underground bulbs and typically produce a single, sturdy stem bearing one cup-shaped or star-shaped blossom. Their smooth, fleshy leaves grow in a soft green hue clustered near the base of the plant. Depending on the cultivar, the petals can range from smooth and rounded to fringed, ruffled, or multi-layered like peonies.

Planting & Care Insights

To enjoy a vibrant spring display, tulip bulbs must be planted in the autumn before the ground freezes. They require a period of cold winter dormancy to trigger proper blooming. Tulips thrive best in locations offering full morning sun paired with exceptionally well-drained, sandy soil, as soggy earth can easily damage the bulbs over the winter months.
