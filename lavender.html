All About Lavender

Lavender is a highly valued perennial shrub belonging to the mint family, Lamiaceae. Native to the Mediterranean region, it is celebrated worldwide for its soothing aroma, beautiful spikes of purple flowers, and silver-green foliage.

Aromatic & Practical Uses

The name lavender comes from the Latin word lavare, meaning "to wash." Historically, ancient Romans used lavender in their baths for its fresh scent and purifying qualities. Today, it is a staple ingredient in global aromatherapy, perfumery, and artisanal crafts, prized for its calming fragrance and beautiful dried floral arrangements.

Botanical Growth Habit

Lavender plants grow as compact, woody shrubs with narrow, needle-like leaves. During the summer months, they produce upright flower spikes packed with small, delicate blossoms. While classic lavender purple is the most famous hue, certain cultivars also bloom in soft shades of pink, white, or deep blue-violet.

Cultivation & Sunlight Tips

Lavender thrives in conditions that mimic its Mediterranean origins: hot sun and lean, rocky soil. It is incredibly drought-tolerant once established and actually prefers poor soil over highly fertilized dirt. The most critical requirement for growing lavender successfully is exceptional soil drainage, as damp roots will quickly damage the plant.
